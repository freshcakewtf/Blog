## Where it started

I remember standing on a rooftop bar in Stamford, CT, bragging to a buddy I’d just met about my "influencer" career in the making. We were talking about startup life when he mentioned how cool my work could be on GIPHY—the platform he worked on. 

At the time, becoming a social media influencer seemed like a perfectly viable and stable plan for a creative. I thought I’d found the shortcut: no more gallery gatekeepers, just me and the masses! (You know, aside from the Algorithm and ads filling up your feed).

So, that week, I posted my first legit GIF to GIPHY. It was nothing special—just a simple animated "LOL" using an illustration I made and the Flow Motion Effect in After Effects with a little wiggle. The next morning, I woke up to millions of views. The most I had ever seen with my work. I spent the whole day pacing my apartment, proudly texting friends and telling my wife I’d gone viral. It was a huge deal... apparently. 

The internet didn't actually care, of course. The hype died down by the next morning, and my "viral" moment was forgotten by everyone but me. But that moment planted a seed. It unlocked this curiosity: Who were these people watching? What were they doing? And why were they looking at my work?

## What Happened Next

As the years passed, my grand plan to become a social media art star began to deteriorate—and probably for the better. I spent a long time chasing algorithms on every popular platform, trying to get that coveted "K" in my follower count, all while wondering if any of it actually mattered.

I eventually realized there was a new gatekeeper: the platforms themselves.

## Social Media isn't all that Social

Social media has undergone a fundamental shift in recent years. It has moved away from *connection* and toward *consumption*. Instead of being tools to help us talk to each other, they've become engines designed to keep us locked to our screens, feeding us a constant stream of passive entertainment. We are spectators, scrolling past content rather than actually engaging with the people behind it.

I felt my work being scrolled past, but rarely *used*. I wasn't building a community; I was just feeding a feed for no reason.

I realized I wanted to meet people where they actually are: in their private group chats and DMs. I wanted to move into a space where my work wasn't just something to be "consumed" passively, but something that helps people express themselves in their most personal conversations.

The data actually backs this up. A [Pew Research study](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/10/07/how-do-americans-engage-with-news-online/) released this October found that 56% of Americans had privately chatted about or shared news online in the past week, while only 26% posted about it publicly. And in a 2025 survey by [Giphy](https://www.axios.com/2025/10/21/giphy-group-chats-gen-z-survey), 90% said they share major life updates with their group chat before posting on social media.

People are just as social as they've ever been; the conversation has just moved into the DMs.

## It's not a Hobby Anymore

This is why I lean into GIPHY. It’s a platform that allows me to publish once and somehow be everywhere—from Instagram to Slack. Other providers like Tenor have done this for years, and now we see new players like KLIPY entering the space.

These platforms give creatives a way to move from being "content creators" to being active participants in a real conversation.

I felt the weight of this a few years ago while sitting at a bar with my buddy Mike. He looked down at a notification on his phone and laughed at a message from his parents. It was an image of the family dog with a thick turkey sticker over it. My Turkey sticker! They didn't know I had made the illustration; they just used it to share a moment of humor.

In that moment, I knew: the place to be wasn't in a public feed lost behind an algorithm. It was in the places where people actually socialize. A place where I could contribute to a shared moment.

At the time of writing this, I have amassed 3.5 billion views on GIPHY. I still don't fully understand the scale of that number, and there are others with far more impressive views, but I know what it means. Somewhere, someone just used a piece of my work to have a shared moment with a friend in a chat. 

And that's all that matters to me. I hope they're having a great conversation.

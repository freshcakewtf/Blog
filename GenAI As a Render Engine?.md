### I think I've been thinking about it wrong

I've been using generative AI tools since, you know, they landed at the intersection of art and tech. 😆 At first it felt like magic. Then it started feeling like a tool. And now I've landed somewhere more familiar. Kind of a full circle moment.

**It's a render engine.**

Not in a "lol just press generate" way. In the actual pipeline sense. The same way Cinema 4D renders what you've modeled and lit. The same way After Effects composites what you've built. GenAI takes what you've designed — your character, your scene, your vision — and renders it.

And that re-frame changed everything for me.

## The pigeon that broke it open

Recently I built a fully realized scene around an unhinged pigeon. Cinematic lighting, the whole thing.

Here's what the pipeline looked like:

- **Project Neo:** modeled the base, focused on proportions and camera angle
- **Firefly Boards + Gemini 3.1:** generated the rendered image from that model
- **Photoshop:**  color, retouching, cleanup
- **Firefly + Luma Ray 3:** video pass
- **After Effects:**  compositing and final touches

Full 3D pipeline. Top to bottom. Modeling, texture and lighting, animation, compositing, editing.

Just with some different tools doing parts of the job.

## The skills didn't change

Back in 2014, I opened Cinema 4D for the first time on a WWE project. No tutorials. No safety net. Just a deadline and a 3D scene I had to figure out on the fly.

For the next six years, C4D was deep in my pipeline. Then around 2020, life happened — dad stuff, work stuff — and 3D took a backseat.

Fast forward to now, dusting those skills off and combining them with the generative workflow I've been building on the 2D side.

And here's what's wild: **every skill I used came from those six years with C4D.** GenAI didn't give me those. Years of making stuff did.

Even if I'm using image models to texture and light a scene, I still need to understand *how* to texture and light a scene to get anything meaningful back.

It's not where the work happens. It's where the work **gets rendered.**

---

## What this means for creative skills

We keep hearing that GenAI is going to replace. In my experience, it's doing the opposite.

Foundational skills are what make these tools actually useful. Without that, you're just pressing generate and hoping.

3D isn't dead. 2D isn't dead. Illustration, motion design, hand-drawn animation — none of it is dead. If anything, the more fluent you are in the fundamentals, the more these tools unlock.

Because you're not prompting from nothing. You're rendering something you actually built.

---

## Where I'm at now

With the pace things are moving — tools like Kling 3.0 pushing what's possible in video generation — I'm thinking about GenAI less like a novelty and more like a new class of software in the same family as the renderers I've always used.

Bring your character designs, your sketches, your models, your textures. The tools meet you there. The thinking is the same. The process is familiar.

Just a new tool doing one part of the job.
